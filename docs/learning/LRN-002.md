# Changing your mind mid-project — and why it's cheap early

This cycle you changed your mind: the app was going to be shared between two
people, then you decided it should be just for you. That's completely normal —
and it's worth understanding how it was handled, because *when* you change your
mind changes how much it costs.

## Key Concepts

**Changing your mind is not a failure — it's information.** You learned
something (you didn't actually want the sharing) and acted on it. Good projects
expect this.

**The cost depends on *when*, not *whether*.** Changing the plan *before* you
build is nearly free. Changing it *after* you build is expensive (you throw work
away). So the goal is to catch changes of mind early.

**A change is recorded, not hidden.** Instead of quietly editing things, the
change was written down as a **decision** (DEC-002) with the reasons and the
consequences. That way the project's history still makes sense later.

**A replan keeps everything consistent.** After the decision, the plan, the task
list, and the project description were all updated together — so nothing was left
pointing at the old direction.

**Two ways to adjust a plan:**
- *Tactical* — a small adjustment to the current cycle (what we did: swapped the
  hosting goal for a rebuild goal).
- *Strategic* — opening a whole new cycle with new goals.

## How This Applies to Your Project

- Your change of mind is recorded in `.gse/decisions.md` as **DEC-002**.
- Your project description (`docs/intent.md`) has a **Revision** note explaining
  what changed and what it retired.
- Cycle 2's plan was adjusted *tactically* — hosting dropped, a rebuild goal
  added — and that adjustment is logged in the sprint archive
  (`docs/sprints/sprint-02/plan-summary.md → Scope Changes`).
- Because the change landed **before** any code was written, nothing was wasted:
  the sprint delivered exactly one goal, cleanly.
